Meowdoku: Rules, Strategy and a Free Practice Puzzle

Meowdoku is a cat-themed logic puzzle from Oakever Games that blends Sudoku-style line thinking with Minesweeper-style deduction. You place one cat in every coloured region, one per row and column, and never let two cats touch — not even at a corner. This independent guide explains the rules, collects practical solving techniques and includes a free browser puzzle generator you can play right now.

The three rules

  1. One cat per coloured region. Every colour is a territory, whatever its shape. Exactly one tile in each territory holds a cat.
  2. One cat per row and column. A cat blocks the rest of its row and its column.
  3. No touching. Two cats may not share an edge or a corner. Every placement also blocks the eight surrounding tiles.

Those three rules interact. A region that sits entirely inside one column claims that column. A row that already contains a cat cannot host another. And a candidate tile that touches an existing cat is dead even if its colour still needs a cat. Most of the game is reading those interactions in the right order — see the strategy guide.

Players often compare it to “Queens” logic puzzles: one piece per row, column and colour, with the no-touching rule on top. If you have solved Queens-style boards before, the first Meowdoku levels will feel familiar.

Frequently asked questions

What is Meowdoku?

Meowdoku is a cat-themed logic puzzle by Oakever Games. You place one cat in every coloured region, one per row and column, and cats may not touch — not even diagonally.

Who makes Meowdoku?

Oakever Games publishes the official app on Google Play, the Galaxy Store and Windows via Google Play Games on PC.

Is Meowdoku free?

Yes. The app is free with ads and optional in-app purchases.

Is there an official Meowdoku web version?

No. The official product is the Oakever Games app. The playable board on this site is an independent practice game with original puzzles, not the official app.

How many mistakes can you make in Meowdoku?

Three per level — the game shows three hearts.

What is the difference between Meowdoku and a Queens puzzle?

Both use one piece per row, column and coloured region. Meowdoku adds the rule that cats cannot touch, including diagonally.
